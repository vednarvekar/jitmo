# Offers lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The tech is real and cheap to run: rules first, models only when needed, 14 MB idle. That keeps delivery cost near zero, which makes a generous guarantee affordable later.
- "Acts, does not chat" is a clear promise of effort removed. Effort and sacrifice is the one value-equation term this can win.
- Privacy-restricted fields (law, finance, clinics) are the one candidate where the pain is a hard ban, not a preference. Open question whether they are reachable and have budget.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No market chosen.** No buyer, no pain, no price. By this lens that is not an offer yet, it is a feature list. Three candidate markets, none tested.
2. **Competing with free.** Copilot is bundled or cheap. A free personal tier plus a paid tier "later" means the only difference is price, and you lose that to Microsoft.
3. **Likelihood is weak.** Router 81-92%, extractor about 63%, chains about 60%, all on the dev PC. A buyer who sees one wrong action in three stops believing it works. Add a delete/move skill and one failure costs real trust.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Name one person who has this pain today and what they currently do about it. Have you spoken to them?
- What dollar or hour result does this deliver, and in what time? "Saves clicks" is not an outcome.
- What proof can a stranger see in the first five minutes that it works?
- Which market candidate pays, and is the buyer the same person who installs it? Open question.
- What guarantee could you give, and what does a refund cost you?
- If you doubled the price, would the buyer still choose this over Copilot? Why?
- How does install and code signing look to a cautious IT buyer? Unresolved.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Pick one market before more building, and talk to at least a handful of real buyers in it. Write down their words.
2. State the dream outcome in one sentence with a time attached, then test it as a pre-sale or paid pilot at a real price.
3. Reach reliability the buyer can feel on the target 8 GB laptop, measured, before charging.
4. Build a stack for that one market (skills, setup done for them, bonuses) and a guarantee the numbers can afford.
5. Drop "free tier to spread installs" until a paid buyer exists.
