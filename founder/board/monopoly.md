# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- Real engineering instinct: a cheapest-first ladder where most requests need no model, 14 MB idle, low-priority helper. Efficiency is a genuine, checkable edge.
- "Acts, does not chat" is a distinct posture from answer-engines.
- Local-first is a true secret-adjacent angle for buyers who have banned cloud AI (law, finance, clinics). That is the only segment where the claim has teeth.
- Honest measurement. The founder reports a failed planner and a 63% extractor instead of hiding them.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No small market, no secret.** Three candidate customers, none chosen. Nothing here starts with a big share of anything. "Privacy and speed on modest hardware" is a feature a platform owner can ship. The secret (what you know that others do not) is not stated.
2. **Not 10x better; arguably worse.** Router 81-92%, extractor 63%, chains about 60%, planner failed. A wrong action (delete, move, send) costs more than a wrong answer. Against bundled Windows Copilot, it is incremental at best. Nothing measured shows a 10x edge on something buyers care about.
3. **Distribution is blank.** No answer to how the first 100 customers arrive or what each costs. Code signing unresolved, so install trust on Windows (SmartScreen) is an open blocker. A free tier "to spread installs" with no channel is not distribution.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which single customer, by name of role and city, would pay first, and have you spoken to five of them?
- What do you know about that customer that Microsoft does not?
- What does the target buyer do today, and what does it cost them? (Open question; no figures given.)
- What stops a copycat or a Copilot update in month six?
- What happens when it acts wrongly on a real file? What is the measured wrong-action rate?
- Why now: what changed that makes small local models enough?
- Do results hold on the 8 GB target laptop? Only the dev PC has been measured.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Pick one narrow segment (the privacy-restricted one is the best fit for the claim) and interview at least a handful before more code.
2. Show a measured, repeatable win against Copilot for that segment on a defined task set, on the 8 GB laptop.
3. Hit a stated accuracy bar on destructive actions, with confirmation and Undo proven.
4. Name a concrete channel for the first 100 users and a signing route.
5. Get at least one buyer to commit to a real price.
